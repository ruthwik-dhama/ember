# SAME / SAME-R

- SAME flew in the Microgravity Science Glovebox on the ISS twice: SAME-1 during Expedition 15 (2007) and the reflight SAME-2 (also called SAME-R) during Expedition 24 (July to September 2010). [NTRS 20130000422, Urban et al., AIAA 2012-3536]
- SAME heated small samples of spacecraft materials below their ignition point to make pyrolysis smoke, because early-stage pyrolysis products, not soot from established fires, are the main target for spacecraft fire detection. [NTRS 20130000422]
- SAME-2 materials were Teflon, Kapton, cotton (cellulose lamp wick), silicone rubber and Pyrell; dibutyl phthalate on a porous wick was also tested. [NTRS 20130000422]
- SAME-1 materials were Teflon, Kapton, cellulose, silicone rubber and dibutyl phthalate. [doi:10.4271/2009-01-2468, Urban et al., SAE ICES 2009]
- Air flow past the sample during heating ranged from quiescent to 8 cm/s; 8 cm/s was the baseline, runs went down to 2 cm/s, and 11 tests used no flow. [NTRS 20130000422]
- Smoke was held in an aging chamber for 11 to 1800 s to simulate transport time from the source to a detector. [NTRS 20130000422]
- Most average smoke particle sizes were in the 200 to 400 nm range, and some quiescent cases produced substantially larger particles. [NTRS 20130000422]
- In baseline runs, the unaged diameter of average mass was 101 nm for Kapton, 170 nm for Teflon, 258 nm for lamp wick, 384 nm for Pyrell and 465 nm for silicone. [NTRS 20130000422, Table 1]
- Smoke particles grew as the smoke aged: after 720 s of aging, the diameter of average mass grew to 161 nm for Kapton, 232 nm for Teflon, 398 nm for lamp wick, 539 nm for Pyrell and 619 nm for silicone. [NTRS 20130000422, Table 1]
- Teflon and Kapton smoke particles were very small, which makes them hard to detect with light-scattering detectors such as the ISS smoke detector. [NTRS 20130000422]
- The diameter of average mass was 30 to 40% larger in zero-flow tests than in flow tests, so smoke trapped in an avionics enclosure is expected to be larger than smoke released in ventilated areas. [NTRS 20130000422]
- SAME used the moment method: the zeroth (number), first (diameter) and third (mass) moments, with a lognormal assumption to derive the geometric mean diameter and geometric standard deviation. [Yang et al., Aerosol Sci. Technol. 49 (2015), doi:10.1080/02786826.2015.1025124]
- The prototype two-moment MPASS sensor reduced the relative variance of its response by at least a factor of 2 compared with a conventional single-output sensor, letting it tell a high smoke concentration from a lower concentration of large dust particles. [NTRS 20130000422]
- Detecting small smoke particles like Teflon and Kapton without nuisance alarms from large dust particles is difficult without some particle size discrimination. [NTRS 20130000422]
- In low gravity, the diameter of average mass of the smoke increased on average by a factor of 1.7. [Yang et al., Aerosol Sci. Technol. 49 (2015), doi:10.1080/02786826.2015.1025125]
- In microgravity, smoke transport was dominated by mixing, and detection lags were longer than in normal gravity. [SAME final report, NASA TM / NIST, https://www.nist.gov/node/1863886]
