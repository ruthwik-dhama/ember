# BASS / BASS-II

- BASS-II measured opposed flame spread over PMMA cylinders on the ISS at opposed flows of 0.4 to 8 cm/s and oxygen concentrations of 15% to 21%. [doi:10.1038/s41598-017-18398-4]
- In microgravity, as opposed flow increased, the flame spread rate first increased and then decreased, unlike on Earth. [doi:10.1038/s41598-017-18398-4]
- No flame spread was observed at opposed flow velocities below 0.6 cm/s in microgravity. [doi:10.1038/s41598-017-18398-4]
- Flame spread was sustained at a lower oxygen concentration in microgravity (17%) than in normal gravity (18%). [doi:10.1038/s41598-017-18398-4]
- At 21% oxygen, flame spread was faster in normal gravity; below 19% oxygen it was faster in microgravity. [doi:10.1038/s41598-017-18398-4]
- On Earth the flame induces an upward buoyant flow of about 30 cm/s, so spread is almost independent of forced flow below that speed. [doi:10.1038/s41598-017-18398-4]
- BASS and BASS-II burned a cotton-fiberglass fabric (SIBAL, 75% cotton / 25% fiberglass) in a 7.6 cm square flow duct inside the Microgravity Science Glovebox at 1 atm, with O2 from 16% to 21% and flows from 1 to 55 cm/s. [NTRS 20150008962, Zhao et al. 2015]
- For the fabric in concurrent flow, flames spread more slowly across narrower samples, at lower flow velocities and at lower oxygen percentages. [NTRS 20150008962, Zhao et al. 2015]
- Low-speed quenching of the fabric flames occurred between 1 and 5 cm/s, at higher flow speeds in lower-oxygen atmospheres. [NTRS 20150008962, Zhao et al. 2015]
