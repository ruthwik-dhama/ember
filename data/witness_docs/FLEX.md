# FLEX

- FLEX burned n-heptane and methanol droplets with initial diameters of 1.5 to 5.0 mm, at ambient O2 mole fractions of 0.1 to 0.4, pressures of 0.7 to 3.0 atm, and N2, CO2 or He diluents. [doi:10.1007/s12217-014-9372-2]
- Large n-heptane droplets continued burning as cool flames after the visible flame went out by radiative extinction. [doi:10.1007/s12217-014-9372-2]
- Two-stage (hot then cool) burning was not seen in heptane droplets smaller than about 2.4 mm. [ISS Program Science Office release, "An Old Theory Goes Up in Cool Flames"]
- A condensed vapor cloud forms around the droplet when the cool flame extinguishes. [Nayagam, APS abstract, https://archive.aps.org/oss/2021/b05/1]
- Safety procedures based only on hot flames may be inadequate. [Nayagam, APS abstract, https://archive.aps.org/oss/2021/b05/1]
- Limiting oxygen index (LOI, below which quasi-steady burning stops) in O2/N2 is 0.12 for methanol and 0.15 for heptane droplets. [doi:10.1007/s12217-014-9372-2]
- With CO2 dispersed into air at 1 atm (simulating a suppressant), the LOI rises to about 0.14 for methanol and 0.17 for heptane; with helium it is about 0.14 and 0.15. [doi:10.1007/s12217-014-9372-2]
- FLEX droplet tests ran at ambient pressures from 0.5 to 3 atm; n-decane droplets showed cool-flame burning at 0.5 atm with 14% to 25% oxygen. [Nayagam et al. 2014, NTRS 20140006750]
- A cool flame burns at about 600 degrees Celsius, while a typical candle burns at around 1,400 degrees Celsius. [NASA Glenn release JR12-009, 2012]
