# DAFT / DAFT-2

- DAFT (Dust and Aerosol measurement Feasibility Test) was a risk-mitigation experiment from NASA Glenn and ZIN Technologies supporting SAME, testing whether the P-Trak counter works reliably in long-duration microgravity. [NTRS 20050215648, Sheredy, NASA Research and Technology 2004]
- The P-Trak is a hand-held condensation nuclei counter that counts individual particles as small as 20 nm by condensing isopropyl alcohol onto them and counting them with a laser. [NTRS 20050215648]
- DAFT flew a P-Trak and a DustTrak on ISS Expedition 10, and then a reference dust source on Expedition 13. [NTRS 20070031686, Urban et al., ISDC 2007]
- DAFT sampled undisturbed air at two locations in the US Lab and in the Node. [NTRS 20070031686]
- DAFT gave the first measurement of background smoke-particle levels on the ISS. [NTRS 20070031686]
- Average ISS cabin air was below 0.005 mg/m3 on the DustTrak and below 15 particles/cm3 on the P-Trak. [NTRS 20070031686]
- The ISS cabin was substantially cleaner than the Space Shuttle or a typical office environment. [NTRS 20070031686]
- One DAFT test deliberately generated aerosol by separating Velcro, producing a large transient particle spike, a nuisance source a smoke detector must not alarm on. [NTRS 20070031686]
- With the reference dust, P-Trak and DustTrak readings compared well on the first run, before low alcohol in the P-Trak affected the results. [NTRS 20070031686]
- DAFT showed the modified P-Trak works in low gravity, clearing it for use in SAME. [NTRS 20070031686]
- The ISS smoke detector uses near-IR forward scattering and is most sensitive to particles larger than about 1 µm, while the Shuttle ionization detector rejected particles larger than 1 to 2 µm. [NTRS 20070031686]
